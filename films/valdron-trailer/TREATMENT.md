# VALDRON — Official Vertical Teaser Treatment

- **Format**: 1080×1920 vertical (9:16), 24 fps, mastered to −14 LUFS (`grain=0` to preserve crisp pixel edges).
- **Runtime**: **120.00 s** original pixel-art trailer body (`t = 0.00–120.00`), preceded by the **9.00 s** Lumrook Game Studio intro (`lumrook-intro.mp4`) formatted seamlessly for 9:16 → **129.00 s (`02:09`) total deliverable**.
- **Language & Voice Policy**: English only, **no voiceover**. Sparse synchronized English text cards (~24% of runtime) locked to musical bars and sound transitions, leaving >85 s of pure visual observation, environmental dread, and uncanny motion.
- **Visual Medium**: Custom nocturnal dark-fantasy pixel art (`270×480` native indexed framebuffer scaled ×4 nearest-neighbour to `1080×1920`), combining ordered 4×4 Bayer dithering, integer-stepped palette ramps, and protected curse-green indices.

---

## 1. Three Candidate Structures

1. **The Silence After Victory (Chosen)** — Begin inside the warm, loud victory celebration of Valdron over fallen Loris; follow a lone knight out into the moonlit hills to sleep; hold on a silent nocturnal palette collapse across the distant kingdom; wake into an abandoned, fog-choked city smeared with blood and green ichor; escalate from eerie domestic investigation and slow body-horror discovery into the wider cursed woods, villages, relics, and creatures, ending before the King's Castle beneath a colossal cyan moon.
   - *Why chosen*: Preserves the knight's first-person experiential discovery, maximizes the emotional shock between warm triumph and dead silence, and protects the mystery of Aleister's identity and the final confrontation.
2. **Fragments in the Fog** — Open cold inside the deserted streets of Valdron, intercutting single-bar flashbacks of the victory feast with mutated silhouettes in the present.
   - *Why rejected*: Gives away the horror state at frame one and weakens the quiet dread of waking up to a changed world.
3. **The Road of Relics** — Open on the distant royal castle and frame the 120 seconds as an escalating dungeon march through villages, swamps, and bosses.
   - *Why rejected*: Reads like an action montage rather than psychological dark-fantasy tragedy and uncanny observation.

## 2. Logline & Emotional Arc

- **Logline**: After the Kingdom of Valdron celebrates the annihilation of Loris, a lone knight wakes outside the city walls to find his homeland utterly silent, steeped in creeping green rot, and populated by things that used to be human.
- **Arc**:
  - **Setup (`0–20 s`)**: Warmth and complicity — banners burn, victors drink, and one weary knight steps outside the walls to sleep beneath the moon.
  - **Turn (`20–68 s`)**: Silent catastrophe — in dead silence a distant green flare drains every light in Valdron; waking to fog, green ichor, and empty homes, the knight realizes a motionless townsfolk silhouette has bent beyond human anatomy.
  - **Escalation & Ending (`68–120 s`)**: Resolve in the unknown — forced through haunted woods, blighted villages, ancient forges, and towering horrors, the knight reaches the valley below the King's Hall under a vast cyan moon where the curse's source still waits.

## 3. Benchmarks (Grammar Only)

- **Benchmark A — *Castlevania: Symphony of the Night* & *Blasphemous* (gothic pixel staging) + Classic *Resident Evil* (1996, stillness & doorway dread)**:
  - *What to learn*: Vertical architectural silhouettes, deep midnight-blue and stone ramps, holding on an empty room two seconds longer than comfortable, letting posture and proportion carry horror without cheap jump-scare zooms.
  - *What not to take*: Specific monsters, crests, UI frames, gothic sprites, typefaces, or melodies.
- **Benchmark B — *Over the Garden Wall* (unknown nocturnal woods) + *Hide (slow)* / *Dark Fantasy* (musical mood references named by user)**:
  - *What to learn*: Layered foreground silhouettes framing mist, fragile detuned keyboard nostalgia curdling into brooding dark-synth pulse, acoustic space around solitary footsteps.
  - *What not to take*: Any melodic intervals, chord transcriptions, synth patches lifted from recordings, or character designs.
